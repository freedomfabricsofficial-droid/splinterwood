# Splinterwood — Art Style Guide & Image Pre-Prompt

The single source of truth for generating enemy and item art. Paste the relevant
**pre-prompt block** before your subject every time you ask an image generator
(ChatGPT / DALL·E, etc.) for a new asset. Built from the three reference drawings
(Hungover Goblin, Bridge Troll, Off-Season Brigand) so the whole bestiary and item
set stays visually identical across hundreds of images.

> The look in one line: **a loose, wobbly black-marker doodle — crude and childlike
> but confident and full of character — pure black ink on nothing, no color, no gray.**

---

## 1. Copy-paste PRE-PROMPT — ENEMIES / CREATURES

```
Create a hand-drawn black-ink illustration of a single fantasy creature, in this EXACT style:

• Pure black line art (#000000) on a TRANSPARENT background. Black only — absolutely
  no color, no gray tones, no gradients, no soft/airbrushed/digital shading, no fills
  of large areas. Interiors stay empty/white.
• Drawn to look hand-made with a black marker or Sharpie: loose, slightly wobbly,
  uneven, organic lines — charmingly crude and childlike, like a talented kid scribbled
  it, but still confident and clearly readable. NOT clean vector lines, NOT polished
  digital art.
• ONE character, full body, centered, facing the viewer (or a slight 3/4 turn),
  standing, with a generous empty margin all around. No background, no scenery, no
  ground line, no cast shadow, no border or frame, no signature.
• Big-headed cartoon proportions: an oversized expressive head and face, with a
  smaller/stubbier or spindly body and limbs. Exaggerated, characterful, a little
  grotesque, and funny.
• Give it a clear ATTITUDE or gag and show its mood with floating cartoon symbols
  ("emanata"): squiggly drunk/stink/dizzy lines, little sparkle/asterisk marks, puffs
  of steam, zigzag/lightning anger bolts, sweat droplets — whatever matches its mood,
  drawn around the head.
• Detailing and the ONLY shading come from hand-drawn marks: short hatching lines,
  cross-hatching, and small dots/dashes — used sparingly for texture (warts, stubble,
  dirt, skin, fabric, wood grain) and for dark spots (e.g. inside sunglasses). Never
  fill anything solid black except tiny things like pupils.
• Clothing is ragged, scrappy and poor: jagged torn hems, square cloth patches with
  little stitch/cross marks, simple belts and buckles. Feet are bare with claws or
  chunky toes, or worn pointy boots/shoes. Hands can be simple little nubs.
• Line weight: a bolder, consistent outline for the silhouette, with lighter, sketchier
  strokes for inner detail and the floating mood-marks.

THE CREATURE: [describe it here — what it is, its mood/gag, and the prop it holds].
```

Then finish with one line, e.g.:
`THE CREATURE: a hungover goblin in cheap sunglasses, dazed and queasy, holding a moonshine jug labeled "XXX", with drunk squiggles and sparkles around its head.`

## 2. Copy-paste PRE-PROMPT — ITEMS / OBJECTS

```
Create a hand-drawn black-ink illustration of a single object, in this EXACT style:

• Pure black line art (#000000) on a TRANSPARENT background. Black only — no color, no
  gray, no gradients, no soft shading, no solid fills. The object's interior stays white.
• Hand-drawn marker/Sharpie look: loose, wobbly, slightly uneven lines, charmingly
  crude and childlike but clearly readable. Not clean vector, not polished digital art.
• ONE object, centered, shown whole, with a generous empty margin. No background, no
  scenery, no surface/ground, no shadow, no frame.
• Detailing and any shading come ONLY from hand-drawn marks — short hatching,
  cross-hatching, and small dots/dashes — used sparingly for material and texture
  (wood grain, metal nicks, cloth, glass shine as a couple of strokes).
• Optional: a tiny bit of hand-lettered labeling or a small margin scribble/arrow if
  it suits the object (like a field-journal note), in the same wobbly ink.
• Bolder outline for the object's silhouette, lighter strokes for inner detail.

THE OBJECT: [describe it here — what it is, its material, any defining detail].
```

Example finish:
`THE OBJECT: a knobbly wooden club carved from a tree branch, lumpy and uneven, with a few wood-grain lines and a worn grip.`

## 3. Full style spec (for reference and tuning)

**Medium / values.** 100% flat black ink line on transparent paper. There is exactly
one "color": black. The only tonal variation is **line density** — areas read darker
because they have more hatching, never because of gray or fill. If you can see a gray, a
gradient, or a big solid-black shape, it's wrong.

**Line quality.** Looks drawn by hand with a marker: lines wobble, wander, vary slightly
in thickness, sometimes overlap or double back. It should feel quick, loose, and a bit
naive — "five-year-old with a Sharpie" energy — yet the creature is still well-observed
and full of personality. Avoid: ruler-straight lines, perfectly smooth curves, uniform
vector strokes, sterile precision.

**Proportions.** Big head, expressive face, comparatively small or stubby/spindly body.
Chibi-adjacent but allowed to be ugly, lumpy, or grotesque. Silhouette should read
instantly.

**Faces & features (vocabulary to draw from).** Big simple eyes with small dot pupils
(or X-eyes / half-lidded for drunk/tired); big pointy ears, often one drooping; chunky
noses; underbites and a couple of big square teeth; lolling tongues; heavy or angry
brows; freckles/warts/sweat as little dots. Push the expression hard.

**Emanata (mood marks) — a signature of the style.** Always give a creature a mood and
draw it floating around the head:
- Drunk / dazed / sick → loopy squiggles, spirals, a few sparkle/asterisk twinkles.
- Angry / fuming → puffs of steam, short zigzag/lightning bolts, throbbing veins.
- Surprised → exclamation strokes, radiating lines.
- Sweaty / nervous → flying sweat droplets.
- Smug / sparkling → little star/asterisk sparkles.

**Texture marks.** Sparse, hand-drawn only: small dots and short dashes for stubble,
warts, dirt, freckles; short parallel hatching for soft shadow or fur; cross-hatching
for genuinely dark spots (sunglass lenses, deep shadow); a few straight-ish lines for
wood grain or metal. Keep most of the figure clean white inside the outline.

**Clothing & gear motif (keeps the world consistent — these are poor, scrappy folk).**
Ragged torn hems drawn as jagged zigzags; square patches stitched with little crosses;
simple belts with a buckle; crude daggers, clubs, sacks, jugs, tools. Bare clawed/three-
toed feet or worn pointy boots. Mitten-simple hands.

**The gag.** Every enemy is a deadpan fantasy joke (a *hungover* goblin, a *toll-
collecting* troll, an *off-season* brigand). Read the creature's name/flavor and draw
the joke: the right prop + the right expression + the right mood marks.

**Composition.** Single subject, centered, full body (whole object for items), facing
forward or slight 3/4, standing/neutral. Leave ~10–15% empty margin on all sides.
Nothing else in the frame.

**Background.** None. Transparent. No ground, horizon, shadow, vignette, or border.

## 5. Worked examples

Enemies:
- `a hungover goblin in cheap sunglasses, dazed and queasy, clutching a moonshine jug labeled "XXX", drunk squiggles and sparkles spinning around its head.`
- `a fat angry bridge troll with a furrowed brow and underbite, hoisting a lumpy wooden club over one shoulder, steam puffs and zigzag anger bolts around its head.`
- `a small scrappy hooded brigand with big round eyes and buck teeth, a tied coin sack in one hand and a dagger tucked in its patched belt, wearing pointy worn boots.`
- `a disappointed stone golem, blocky and cracked, shoulders slumped, sad eyes and a downturned mouth, a single sweat-drop of resignation.`
- `a smug tax collector, tall and thin in a tall hat, clutching a ledger and pointing one bony finger, a couple of self-satisfied sparkles nearby.`

Items:
- `a knobbly wooden club carved from a branch, lumpy with a few wood-grain lines and a worn grip.`
- `a small round healing tonic in a corked bottle, a couple of bubble dots inside and two short shine strokes on the glass.`
- `a chunky iron pickaxe with a nicked, dented head and a wrapped handle.`
- `a fat coin purse pulled shut with a drawstring, a little stitched patch and a faint "¢" scratched on the side.`

## 6. Hard rules — NEVER do these

- No color of any kind. No gray. No gradients. No soft/blurred/airbrushed shading.
- No large solid-black fills (only tiny pupils etc.). Interiors stay white.
- No background, scenery, ground line, cast shadow, vignette, frame, or border.
- No clean vector lines or slick polished digital rendering — it must look hand-drawn.
- No realism, 3D, painterly rendering, anime screentones, or photo style.
- No extra text, captions, or watermark (a tiny in-world label on an object is the only
  exception).
- One subject only, centered.

---

## 7. Technical output

- **Square canvas (1:1)**, generated large — 1024×1024 or higher — for crisp downscaling.
- **Transparent background (PNG with alpha).** Ask the generator for transparent
  explicitly. If it can only produce a solid background, request "plain flat pure-white
  background, no shadow" and have the white removed afterward — the game places these on
  aged parchment, so a white box would look wrong; transparent black-line art reads as
  ink on paper.
- Subject centered with ~10–15% padding.
- **File name = the asset's id, lowercase** (e.g. `brigand.png`, `taxman.png`,
  `golem.png` for enemies; the item's id for items).

---

## 8. Consistency tips for hundreds of assets

- Paste the pre-prompt block **every time** — generators don't remember between images.
- **Attach one or two of the approved reference drawings** to the generation request as
  a style reference whenever the tool allows it; this locks the look far better than
  words alone. (Re-use the goblin + troll as your anchors.)
- Keep framing identical run to run: same full-body, centered, forward-facing setup.
- Drive variety through the *creature and its gag*, never through the rendering — the
  rendering is always this exact black-marker doodle.
- When a result drifts (color creeps in, lines get too clean, a background appears),
  regenerate and add the matching line from "NEVER do these."
