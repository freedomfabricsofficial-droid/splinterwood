The Splinterwood Ledger

Progression Plan — making the game one players can't put down

The thesis

Splinterwood Ledger today has a strong aesthetic identity, distinctive voice, good multi-skill interdependency, helpers-as-automation, and a satisfying modifier-and-tier roll system. What it doesn't have is a reason to play tomorrow. This plan adds the four pillars common to every long-running idle game on Steam — prestige, exponential scaling, a visible medium-term hook, and surprise unlocks — while keeping everything that already works.

What's already working (don't touch)

Aesthetic and voice

  • Hand-drawn pencil-sketch ledger style is genuinely distinctive in the genre
  • Maggie's character, item flavor text, and the writing have personality — most idle games are voiceless

Existing mechanics worth preserving

  • Multi-skill interdependency (woodcutting feeds carving)
  • Helpers as automation
  • Tier + modifier roll system — variable-reward dopamine works
  • Story bible: the loop-in-hell premise is exactly the right frame for a prestige system

What's missing (the problems to solve)

Gap #1 — No prestige / no compounding loop

The game progresses linearly: gain XP → level up → unlock more. There's no reset mechanic that trades progress for permanent multipliers. Without this, players hit a wall and have no answer to "why keep playing?"

The Daily Bread buffs are a baby version of this but they trickle in from streak claiming, not from gameplay choices.

Gap #2 — Numbers don't go exponential

Tier 2 gives ~2× tier 1; tier 3 gives ~3×. Linear-ish. Idle game players come for the exponential growth fantasy — going from 100 coin/hour to 100 million coin/hour over the course of play. Linear progression eventually feels like a chore.

Gap #3 — No medium-term hook

Why log in tomorrow? Right now: claim Daily Bread + collect a letter + chip away at the same tasks. Compare to Melvor ("today I'll hit Lv 70 Smithing and unlock Adamant gear") or NGU ("today my titans clear boss 12"). Splinterwood needs a clear "what am I working toward right now" answer at all times.

Gap #4 — No surprises

New tier of tree = harder tree. New foe = harder foe. The structure repeats with bigger numbers but no new mechanics revealed. What hooks players at the 10-hour mark in NGU and similar is "wait, I can do that now?" Splinterwood's story bible hints at mechanical surprises (Maggie's reveal, Laileb, the floor 4 trapped soul) but they aren't tied to gameplay-changing unlocks.

The plan

Five phases, ordered by impact. Each phase has a goal and a list of things to build. Specifics (numbers, formulas, exact unlocks) get decided when we get to each phase. The order matters: don't start phase 3 until phase 1 and 2 are landed and tested.

Phase 1 — Next up

Remove level caps and convert to exponential growth

Foundation work that everything else depends on. Without exponential scaling, prestige multipliers won't feel meaningful and the game stays linear.

  • Remove the 99-level cap on all skills
  • Rebalance the level cost curve so progression remains satisfying at high levels
  • Convert XP rewards, coin gains, and combat numbers to scale exponentially with skill level
  • Update UI to handle big numbers (1.4M, 23.7B, etc.) cleanly
  • Verify the existing stack-compressed inventory and adaptive offline tick survive the new scaling

Success criteria: a player at level 200 sees numbers orders of magnitude beyond a level 20 player, and the curve still feels rewarding to climb.

Phase 2

Build a prestige / loop system

The biggest single fix. The story bible is already about being trapped in repeating loops in hell — the mechanics should mirror it. This is where Splinterwood gets its long-term hook.

  • Define what "completing a loop" means narratively and mechanically (kill a final-floor boss? Reach a milestone? Specific quest?)
  • Design the prestige currency: what is it called, how is it earned, what does it represent in fiction
  • Decide what carries over between loops (permanent upgrades, certain items, story flags, equipment) and what resets (skills? coin? all of it?)
  • Design the prestige shop / tree: what can the player buy with prestige currency
    • XP gain multipliers (global and per-skill)
    • Coin find / drop rate multipliers
    • Starting helpers already hired for the next loop
    • Skip the early game (start at higher base level)
    • Unlock new mechanics in subsequent loops (loop 1 has 3 skills, loop 2 unlocks a fourth)
    • Aesthetic rewards (new Maggie letter style, new margin doodles)
  • Always show the prestige preview before commit: "After this loop, your gather speed will be 4× permanently"
  • Write the narrative around the first prestige — Maggie's reveal, what the loop means, why the player is doing this again

Success criteria: the player completes their first loop, sees a tangible multiplier on the next run, and feels eager to start again.

Phase 3

Add exponential scaling on top of current numbers

Even before prestige is unlocked, the player should feel their power compounding inside a single run. Two layers stack to give this feel.

  • Build a "Total Multiplier" or similar tracked stat that grows with playtime / total XP / total kills, visible somewhere prominent
  • Build skill synergies: every N levels of woodcutting grants a permanent X% bonus to carving XP, and so on for every meaningful skill pair
  • Display these multipliers in the player's stats so they can see them growing
  • Audit existing balance to confirm the new scaling doesn't trivialize early content

Success criteria: a player can point to a number on their screen and say "look how much stronger I've gotten in the last hour."

Phase 4

Make the medium-term goal explicit in the UI

Players should always know what they're working toward without having to think about it.

  • Add a persistent "Next Unlock" or "Currently Pursuing" element somewhere prominent
  • Shows the next thing the player will gain (next tier unlock, next quest milestone, next prestige threshold, next mechanic) with current progress
  • Updates dynamically based on game state — never empty, never stale
  • Optional: list of secondary goals the player has unlocked but not finished

Success criteria: ask any player "what are you working on right now?" and they can answer in one sentence.

Phase 5

Front-load surprises in the first 5 hours

The first hour is the retention bottleneck. The first five hours determine whether the player will still be playing in week two. Add one "wait, what?" moment every hour for the early game.

  • Hour 1 surprise — something the player didn't expect (could be: a second resource discovered mid-task, a letter with a surprise gift, a foe drops something unprecedented, a new UI element unlocks)
  • Hour 3 surprise — bigger reveal (a hidden NPC speaks, a new mechanic appears, a story moment lands)
  • Hour 5 surprise — meta-level reveal that re-frames the game (per story bible, Maggie's mask starts slipping, time inconsistencies become visible)
  • Bake these into the existing quest / journal / story-letter systems — don't bolt them on as separate things

Success criteria: a player at the 5-hour mark has had at least three "huh, didn't expect that" moments and is curious about what's next.

Phase 6 — Polish

Tighten the first five minutes

Even more critical than the first hour. The player decides whether to keep the game open within the first 5 minutes of opening it.

  • Compress the first level-up. The first XP bar should fill fast enough to feel rewarding
  • Add micro-progression: visible per-task progress bars, "first cut" / "first kill" achievements, milestone toasts
  • Make the first 5 minutes feel like things are constantly happening — coins appearing, levels ticking up, new items rolling in
  • Audit the very first quest to ensure it lands within minute 2 max

Success criteria: a brand new player at minute 5 has leveled up at least twice, seen a roll above regrettable, and feels the loop clicking.

What we won't do

  • No microtransactions or paywalls. The genre's loudest complaint. Premium one-time pricing on Steam is the right model.
  • No piling on systems at once. Each new mechanic should land alone, with space to breathe, ideally tied to a prestige unlock so it feels earned.
  • No late-game requiring active management. NGU's mixed reception comes from this — half the audience bounces when an "idle" game demands a check-in every 20 minutes.
  • No prestige resets without preview. Always show the player exactly what they're getting before they commit.

How we'll work through this

  1. Phase by phase, in order. Don't start phase N+1 until phase N is in the build and tested.
  2. At the start of each phase: spec it out together. Decide specifics. Write it. Test it.
  3. Each phase produces a real version bump that's playable end-to-end.
  4. Revisit this doc at the start of each phase — adjust if reality contradicts the plan.
