# The Splinterwood Ledger — Story Bible

> **PRIVATE DESIGN DOCUMENT — NOT FOR PLAYERS**
> This is the ground truth of what the game is *actually* about. Players
> should not see this. It exists so we don't contradict ourselves when
> dripping clues, and so future content lines up with the same answers.
>
> When in doubt about whether a line is "too revealing," check this doc.
> When designing a new NPC, check this doc.
> When writing a journal page, check this doc.
>
> Last updated: v0.37 (story bible established, pre-implementation)

---

## The Premise (One Sentence)

The player is a damned soul trapped in a hell loop disguised as an idle
fantasy RPG, every NPC except possibly one is a tormentor whose job
is to keep the player working, building, fighting, and the warm friendly
innkeep Maggie who has been the player's only friend is the architect
of the entire prison and the final boss.

---

## The Core Frame

### What the player thinks is happening
- They're an accountant who got mistaken for an adventurer
- They arrived in Splinterwood, met Maggie the friendly innkeep
- They're working their way through floors of a fantasy world
- Each floor has new skills to master, new gear, new foes
- Eventually they'll be powerful enough to do… something. It's vague.
- Maggie writes them letters. They're a regular fixture in this world now.

### What is actually happening
- The player is dead, or at minimum: not real in the human sense
- The "world" is a constructed hell-pocket designed to keep a soul busy
- Every NPC the player meets is either a demon assigned to a role
  ("the innkeep," "the smith," "the helper-coordinator") or another
  trapped soul like the player
- **Maggie is the architect of this specific loop.** She is also the
  final boss. She is one tormentor among many — she has colleagues
  doing this elsewhere with other souls and other personas. She
  chose "warm friendly innkeep" for this particular player because
  it works on them.
- "Skills" are not progress. They're the rhythm of torment — repetitive
  labor disguised as growth. Every level-up is the system tightening
  its grip while the player thanks it.
- The "ledger" the player keeps is the demonic record of their own
  suffering, kept by the player themselves because that's part of the
  cruelty. Maggie's "Counter" buffs are debits and credits in this record.
- The 5 floors are concentric circles of the loop. Each floor is the
  player accepting deeper labor, further removal from any memory of
  who they were.
- The final encounter reveals everything: Maggie drops the persona,
  the player fights her, and is reset. This is the prestige mechanic.

## The Hierarchy of Knowing

This is critical for consistent writing. Different characters know
different amounts.

### Maggie — The Architect & Final Boss
- Knows everything.
- **She is the architect of THIS loop, assigned to torment THIS player
  specifically.** She is one tormentor among many. There are other
  Maggies running other loops for other souls — each specialized in
  a particular persona that works on a particular kind of victim.
  For this player, she chose "warm friendly innkeep." It works.
- Was assigned to this specific player as their primary handler AND
  designer. Splinterwood, Greystone, all of it is structured around
  what will keep THIS player engaged.
- Her warmth, her humor, her affection, her "checking in" — all
  calculated. She is performing a role, the role is effective, and
  she has been doing this a long time.
- The clinginess is surveillance. "How are you, dear?" is intelligence
  gathering, not concern. She uses what she learns to adjust the loop
  in real-time.
- Her flavor text and letters are written to be **funny if naive,
  ominous if you know**. Every existing Maggie line should pass this
  test. Going forward, this is non-negotiable. **She is the final
  boss; she cannot have a single line that breaks character.**
- She does not break character until the pocket dimension.
- Mid-game players might guess; late-game players will know; she
  will never give them the satisfaction of confirming it before the
  reveal.
- She has **colleagues**. The player may glimpse this in the pocket
  dimension — Maggie is a worker, not the Devil. The cosmic scale
  of cruelty implied by her having peers is left for the player to
  contemplate. We never show those colleagues directly.

### Maggie's Post-Reveal Voice
- The shift is sudden and total. The bubbly, loving, clingy Maggie
  disappears completely.
- Replace with: **ice cold, clinical, professional.**
- Speaks like someone reviewing a performance evaluation.
- Drops the affectionate verbal tics ("dear," ALL CAPS emphasis).
- Crisp short sentences. Almost bureaucratic.
- Not cruel in tone. Not mocking. Just *finished* with the
  performance. The performance was for the player; now it's over.
- Possibly tired. She has done this before. With this player.
  Many times.
- (Detailed dialogue/voice for the post-reveal Maggie will be
  drafted closer to when we build the boss encounter. For now,
  the commitment is just: total shift, bubbly → ice cold clinical.)

### Brock — Trapped Soul
- A previous "player" who completed the loop and was reset, but somehow
  retained fragments of awareness
- Doesn't know exactly what happened to him, but knows something is wrong
- His curtness is not personality — it's grief. He has been here too long
  and knows getting close to a new arrival means watching them break.
- 1-5 word responses are the most emotional thing he can manage
- Late game, his lines might lengthen slightly as he recognizes the
  player has started to figure it out
- Cannot help the player directly. He has tried before. The system
  always finds a way to make him forget the next loop. He's not sure
  if his hints will land.
- **He is bound to Greystone Reach. He cannot leave Floor 2.** This is
  one of the mechanical evidences of the constructed nature of the
  world — the player can go up the floors, Brock cannot follow. He
  is *assigned* to that floor the way a tormentor would be assigned
  to one, but unlike the tormentors, he is a soul not a worker.
- The player returning to Greystone after Floor 5 to see him is the
  final story-beat with him. He's been waiting through everything.

### Helpers — Demons in Service Roles
- Each helper has a role-name (Twig-Picker Pim, Doctor Vein, etc.)
- Their wackiness is the trap — players bond with them, accept them
  as charming locals, and never realize they're literally demonic
  service workers whose job is to make the labor seem cooperative
- Each helper has a "complaint" line they say occasionally — these
  read as funny but are actually the demon expressing real
  frustration with their assigned eternal task
- The names follow a pattern: a demeaning descriptor + a name
  (Twig-Picker, Bucket-Tipper, Doctor [who is not a doctor]).
  This is intentional — they're job titles, not names.

### Laileb the Wizard — Bitter Tormentor
- A tormentor, like Maggie, assigned to Floor 3 (the Cloud Islands)
- Has been doing this longer than Maggie. Older worker, lower-ranked,
  passed over for the assignments she got.
- Resents her bitterly. Talks her down constantly the way a passed-over
  employee talks about the favorite.
- Stopped performing the "charm" role years ago. His raunchy/nasty
  remarks are genuine, not strategic. He doesn't bother hiding what he is.
- Despite his decay, the player can sense immense power from him.
  He used to do more important work.
- His bitterness makes him the most accidentally-honest voice about
  Maggie in the game. Everything he says about her is true. The
  player just has to filter the spite out.
- Knows the player is a soul, knows what Maggie is doing, knows the
  whole structure — but only mentions any of it in slips, complaints,
  and jealous asides. Never directly.
- Name etymology: "Laileb" = "Belial" backwards.

### Other NPCs — A Mix
- Most are demons in role
- A few are other trapped souls, indistinguishable unless you know
- Late-game, the player can use environmental clues to start
  identifying which is which
- The player meeting another trapped soul who knows what's happening
  is a key story moment — probably Floor 4

### The Final Boss = Maggie
- See above. Maggie is the architect and the final encounter.
- The "Devil" as a separate entity does not exist in this story.
- If the player imagines a Devil somewhere, that's their projection.
  The architecture of this loop is one tired worker named Maggie.
- The horror is the scale: if Maggie is a worker, there is a
  *company*. The game implies but never shows it.

## What Each Floor Reveals

The drip pace. Players should be able to figure out roughly this much by
end of each floor, if they're paying attention. Casual players might
miss everything until the final reveal — that's fine.

### Floor 1 — Splinterwood (existing)
**What it appears to be:** A starter village. Wood-cutting and carving.
Maggie the friendly innkeep. First combat against a goblin or two.

**What gets dripped:**
- A SINGLE cryptic line per NPC — small, dismissable, won't be noticed
  by casual players
- Maggie occasionally says something that's ALMOST normal but slightly
  off in a way that's hard to pin down
- ONE found journal page from a "previous accountant" with handwriting
  that looks oddly like the player's — page describes Splinterwood in
  the past tense, like the writer is leaving
- An environmental flavor line that occasionally appears in the gather
  log: "The oak's bark looks oddly familiar today."

**What players should feel:** Nothing alarming. Just "this game has
voice." Maybe a faint unease at one or two lines, easily forgotten.

### Floor 2 — Greystone Reach (existing)
**What it appears to be:** Mining and smithing. Brock the curt smith.
Foes scale up.

**What gets dripped:**
- Brock has a moment where he almost says something, then doesn't.
  ("You should— never mind. Sharpen yer pick.")
- A second journal page found in a mining vein — same handwriting as
  the first, but the writer seems more tired. "Maggie checks in
  often. She is very kind. I find I am writing more carefully."
- Maggie's letters occasionally reference things the player didn't
  tell her ("You looked tired yesterday — eat your stew, dear")
- The first time the player dies in combat and gets revived, a single
  line of log text: "You feel like you've done this before."

**What players should feel:** Faint unease. Some players post on Steam
discussions wondering if the game is "trying to be funny or weird."

### Floor 3 — The Cloud Islands (Laileb the Wizard)
**What it appears to be:** A series of floating islands suspended in
endless sky. Real-looking clouds drift past in every direction. There
is no ground anywhere — only the islands. The player crosses between
them via rope bridges, wind-skiffs, or pure leaps of faith.

The third voiced NPC is **Laileb the Wizard.** Ancient, gnarled,
hunched, decrepit. The player can sense immense power from him
despite his obvious physical decay. He is creepy as hell. He makes
raunchy, nasty, inappropriate remarks. He talks badly about Maggie
in the way a child does sour grapes — jealous of her position,
belittling her, slipping information about her constantly because
he can't shut up about her.

**Laileb's role:**
- A tormentor, like Maggie, but a lower-ranked one
- He has been doing this longer than Maggie. He is the bitter
  old-timer who got passed over for the prize assignment. Maggie
  is younger, sharper, more effective. He resents her.
- His raunchy/nasty remarks are real — he stopped performing charm
  long ago. He doesn't bother. Maggie performs. He doesn't.
- His bitterness makes him the most honest voice about Maggie in
  the entire game. Everything he says about her is true. The
  player just has to filter the spite out.
- He teaches the new skills, badly, while complaining
- Name etymology: "Laileb" is "Belial" backwards (the demon of
  worthlessness/lawlessness). Players who notice will smile.

**New skills (paired, like F1/F2):**
- **Alchemy** (gather-style): combining items into potions, tinctures,
  elixirs. Mechanically maps to existing gather skills — completion
  cycles, yields, modifiers on the output.
- **Enchanting** (craft-style): applying magical modifiers to existing
  gear. Mechanically maps to existing craft skills — uses materials
  from Alchemy, produces enhanced equipment.

**What gets dripped:**
- Laileb's lines are the primary drip vehicle here. He complains
  about Maggie's "performance" constantly. Players paying attention
  will start to piece together that Maggie is some kind of
  performer/professional. He never says what she actually is.
- A third journal page found in this floor — same handwriting as
  the first two, but the writer mentions someone "old and bitter"
  who watched them, who whispered things they wish they hadn't
  heard
- Maggie's letters now reference the player traveling. She says
  she misses them. She says she hopes the new acquaintance is
  treating them well. The "checking in" intensifies.
- An environmental log line that occasionally appears during
  Alchemy: "You catch yourself measuring something by a memory
  you don't have."

**What players should feel:** Mildly disturbed. Some players have
started to suspect specific things about Maggie. The theory threads
start showing up.

### Floor 4 — The Labyrinth Crypt
**What it appears to be:** A massive, labyrinthine crypt. Stone
corridors that fold back on themselves. Sconces that flicker without
flame. The player is trapped here in the sense that they can leave
upward (back to F3) but progress requires going deeper, and the
geometry doesn't quite work the way it should.

**New skill (single, not paired):**
- **Reckoning.** Maps mechanically to combat — same shape (foe HP,
  player swings, damage, drops) but with new foe types and harder
  pitch. Lower XP per kill, harder enemies, items that read like
  grave-goods. Foes are echoes, undead, things-that-shouldn't-be.
- The narrative framing: "combat" was hitting wolves and goblins.
  "Reckoning" is fighting things that recognize you. That know your
  name. That have business with you specifically.

**The trapped soul encounter:**
- The player meets another trapped soul somewhere in the crypt.
  Brief, panicked. They speak directly.
- **They are a former player** but this is written ambiguously.
  Their warnings describe someone warm, kind, who "checks in." They
  do not name Maggie. They do not specify their own past clearly.
  Their language uses ambiguous "she was kind to me" / "she will
  be kind to you" phrasings.
- First-time players read this as: "okay, this person also went
  through the loop, they're warning me about a tormentor."
- Returning players (post-rebirth) read this as: "this is literally
  what I'm going to become."
- The soul is interrupted before they can finish what they were
  saying. They are vanished/killed by the system mid-sentence.
- The player cannot save them. There is no choice. This is the
  player's first direct evidence that someone is actively watching
  and intervening.

**What gets dripped:**
- Multiple journal pages from OTHER hands. Different ink, different
  handwriting, but the time-confusion in all of them is identical.
  Pages reference being watched by someone warm and kind. The
  player should start to notice this pattern.
- The Tome's pages section is now unsettling to browse — the pages
  add up to a chorus of similar voices, all having gone through
  similar things.
- Maggie's letters lean hard into the "checking in" thing. She
  references the player "going to dark places." She offers to send
  comforts. The clinginess is now obviously surveillance.
- Environmental log lines during Reckoning combat: "The thing's
  face is wrong but you can't say why." / "Its voice is almost
  familiar." / "You should not be here. You should not be here."

**What players should feel:** They know. Or they're very close.
The remaining question is what to DO about it.

**What players should feel:** They know. Or they're very close. The
remaining question is what to DO about it.

### Floor 5 — The Drowned Library
**What it appears to be:** An endless flooded library. Knee-deep
water everywhere. Pages floating loose. Shelves submerged. Sconces
above the waterline. The light is dim and reflective. Sound carries
strangely.

**The structural twist (mechanical):**
- **No new skill code.** Floor 5 has a skill called **Vigil**, but
  mechanically it reuses existing task types with new flavor.
- **Salvage** task: gather pages from the water. Uses Woodcutting
  math. Longer cycles, fewer-but-meaningful drops (pages and
  curios mostly, very little gear).
- **Reread** task: combat against book-things. Uses Combat math.
  Foes are book-wardens and waterlogged scholars who fight with
  words/memories. New foe types but familiar mechanics.
- The narrative point: the player has no new tricks left. They
  only have what they've already become. The labor is taking away
  agency by not giving them new toys to learn. They are *finished*
  growing.

**Vigil tracker:**
- A new tracker visible on this floor. Increments slowly as the
  player completes Floor 5 tasks.
- When Vigil reaches a threshold (TBD numeric — calibrated to
  player time), the random event for the pocket dimension fires.
- The trigger waits for the next natural action completion within
  ~30 seconds of the threshold. Player is not yanked mid-swing.
- If the player ignores 30s of inactivity, force-trigger.

**The Drowned Archivist (mini-boss):**
- A regular combat encounter on Floor 5. Mechanically familiar.
- Extra-flavorful death dialogue when defeated. Possibly a final
  page drop with significant content.
- Defeating the Archivist is one of the conditions for the Vigil
  tracker to be eligible to fill.

**Brock's final scene:**
- Brock stays in Greystone permanently — he is a trapped soul
  bound to his assigned zone and cannot leave.
- His final scene happens when the player visits him in Greystone
  AFTER Floor 5 is mostly done. The player has to *choose* to go
  back and see him.
- He's been waiting. He acknowledges that he knows. He apologizes
  for not warning the player sooner. He gives the player one
  specific piece of information that prepares them for the boss.
  He cannot say Maggie's name. He gestures back toward Splinterwood.
  His final words are short, but more than 5.
- The player returning to Greystone to find Brock still there,
  still curt across all those hours, is its own quiet horror.
- The player going up the floors and finding Brock unable to
  follow plants the seed of "this place doesn't work the way it
  pretends to."

**What gets dripped:**
- Maggie's letters become almost desperate. She is losing the
  player. The warmth is fear-of-failing-her-assignment now.
- Pages found here are the most fragmented and most distressed.
  Some pages contain only single sentences. Some are torn.
- Environmental log lines: "The water is the same temperature as
  your hands." / "You think you have read this page before." /
  "Something brushes your ankle." / "Nothing brushes your ankle."
- The "completion" prompt appears when Floor 5 is mostly done.
  The player can technically stop here. They've finished what the
  game presented as the journey. But there is something else, if
  they keep pushing.

### The Pocket Dimension — Final Boss (Maggie)
- Triggered by a random event after Floor 5 is complete AND the
  player has maxed combat (player choice on whether to seek it)
- A cave or threshold appears. Player can enter or ignore it.
- Inside: a clean, quiet space. Not hellish-looking. Maggie's inn —
  but emptied. Wrong. Too clean. No fire in the hearth.
- Maggie is there, behind her counter. Still visually Maggie. Still
  smiling. She greets the player normally.
- Then, mid-sentence, the smile drops. The shift is total. She
  becomes ice-cold clinical. Drops "dear." Drops the ALL CAPS.
  Drops the affection. She has been doing this a long time and
  she is *tired*.
- The reveal happens through Maggie's clinical narration. She
  explains the loop briefly. No drama, no monologuing. She's done
  this conversation many times before — possibly with this same
  player.
- **The boss fight: combat against Maggie.** Visually still Maggie
  the innkeep. She fights with whatever an innkeep has — kitchen
  knives, rolling pins, the player's own ledger used against them
  (mechanics TBD). The horror is that the player has to hurt
  someone who, until five minutes ago, was the only kind voice
  in the world.
- Player choice (deferred design): something that affects the
  rebirth. Possibly: how much of Maggie remains after the fight,
  or whether the player chose to fight at all.
- Rebirth: the loop resets. Player retains some persistent
  knowledge / mechanical bonuses. The game continues. Maggie
  returns to her warm performance on the next loop. Whether she
  remembers fighting the player is left ambiguous to the player.

## Mechanical Hooks for the Story

These are the systems we need to build over time to support the
narrative. Some exist; some don't.

### Found Pages
- New item subcategory or curio variant
- Have descriptive flavor text that IS the lore
- Stored in a new Tome section: "Pages"
- Player can browse them in the order they found them
- Some pages may unlock new pages on subsequent gather completions
  (rare drop chain)

### Environmental Log Lines
- During gather/craft tasks, occasionally a flavor line appears in
  the activity log
- 95% of these are tone-appropriate flavor ("The oak gives way
  cleanly today.")
- 5% are cryptic story lines ("You don't remember why you started
  swinging the axe today.")
- Floor-specific lines, can scale frequency by floor

### Curio Items
- Already exist as a category. Currently empty.
- Each curio is a small lore drop with a long flavor description
- Some curios describe things the writer can't fully remember — names
  half-forgotten, places that "used to be" something, objects whose
  purpose is unclear to the page-writer themselves

### NPC Dialogue Pools
- Currently NPCs have static lines (Maggie's letter pool is the
  best-developed example)
- Need: small additional pools of "cryptic" lines that fire less
  frequently. Don't replace the existing humor — augment it.

### Persistent Knowledge (Post-Rebirth)
- Mechanical: bonuses retained across loops
- Narrative: small details persist that shouldn't. A specific
  curio always appears. A specific page is always findable. The
  player has the choice to keep these or "discard" them in the
  rebirth ritual.
- This rewards engaged players who care about the story without
  punishing players who skip the lore.

## Pacing Principles

1. **The first 5-10 hours feel like the game does today.** No
   meaningful story shift. The player should be having fun before
   they earn the depth.

2. **The drip is asymmetric.** Players who pay attention to flavor
   text are rewarded. Players who skip it still get the final reveal.

3. **Maggie never breaks character before the pocket dimension.**
   She is the constant. Her warmth is the thing that keeps players
   bonded to the loop, and that's the point. **Because she is the
   final boss, every single Maggie line is a potential spoiler if
   mishandled.** Every existing line and every future line must
   pass the test: funny if naive, ominous if you know. Never
   ominous in a way that breaks the surface read.

4. **Brock is the foil.** He cannot say what he knows. His curtness
   IS the message — silence as the only honest response in a place
   where everyone else lies.

5. **Lore is never explained, only seen.** No NPC sits the player
   down for exposition. Every clue is small. The player assembles
   the picture themselves.

6. **The reveal is a confirmation, not a surprise.** By the time
   the player meets the Devil, they should already strongly
   suspect. The reveal feels earned, not shocking.

## Writing Style Guide

### General
- Voice-driven, deadpan, slight gallows humor
- Short sentences when something matters
- Specific concrete imagery over abstract feelings
- Avoid the word "soul" until late game — it telegraphs

### Maggie's voice
- ALL CAPS for emphasis (existing)
- Affectionate insults ("you DISASTER")
- "Dear" as a verbal tic
- "I" statements that put her in the player's space
  ("I worried about you yesterday")
- Slight clinginess that reads as warmth — until it doesn't

### Brock's voice
- 1-5 words per response
- Almost never finishes a thought
- Repeats himself when stressed ("Sharpen yer pick. Sharpen it.")
- Occasionally drops a word that doesn't belong in his usual
  vocabulary, hinting at a different past

### Laileb's voice
- Wheezing, sing-song, gross
- Frequent unsolicited opinions, especially about Maggie
- Calls Maggie things like "that one," "the inn-girl," "her with
  the apron"
- Sour-grapes complaints disguised as wisdom: "Oh she's GOOD,
  she's very GOOD, you should see how she's GOOD at the things
  she does..."
- Inappropriate physical comments about no one specific. Vaguely
  raunchy without crossing into actually offensive — like a creepy
  uncle, not a sex pest.
- Frequent self-aggrandizement: "When I was — well, you know who
  you're talking to."
- Slips real information constantly while complaining. The slips
  are the lore drops.
- Calls the player "boy"/"girl"/"thing" depending on his mood,
  rarely a real address term

### Found Pages / Journal voice
- Past tense. Always.
- **NO dates. NO references to specific times.** Time doesn't pass
  linearly here and the writers feel it. Instead of dates, writers
  express confusion about duration: "It has been some time since I
  saw the sun. I don't know how long." / "I can't remember when I
  started writing this." / "I keep meaning to count the days but
  the count never seems to add up."
- References to people the page-writer knew, with names that
  feel familiar but aren't quite right
- Handwriting descriptions ("the ink is darker here")
- Pages become more frantic across the run
- The time-confusion is itself a clue. Players who pay attention
  should notice ALL the page-writers have this same disorientation.

### Environmental Log voice
- Almost identical to normal log lines — a player skimming should
  not notice anything different
- Off-by-one strangeness: a familiar phrase with one word swapped
- Memory-triggers: "today" / "again" / "still" / "remember"

## What NOT To Do

- **Don't reveal "you are in hell" in writing before the boss fight.**
  Players figuring it out is fine. The game stating it is not.
- **Don't make the early game feel grim.** The contrast is the point.
  Splinterwood should feel charming.
- **Don't add jump-scares, screen-flickers, or horror-game tropes.**
  The horror is psychological and slow. No cheap moves.
- **Don't break Maggie's voice.** Even when she's at her most
  ominous, she's still funny. The horror is THAT she's still funny.
- **Don't have NPCs apologize for being mean.** They're not mean.
  They're polite. That's the point.
- **Don't write the reveal as a monologue.** The boss conversation
  should be brief, calm, and devastating.

## Skill Arc — The Progressive Loss of Agency

The skills the player acquires across the 5 floors are designed to
trace an arc from "physical labor" to "abstract reckoning" to
"observation only." The player gets more powerful while having less
to actually do.

- **Floor 1 (Splinterwood):** Woodcutting, Carving, Combat — basic
  physical labor + early combat
- **Floor 2 (Greystone Reach):** Mining, Smithing — physical labor,
  harder pitch
- **Floor 3 (Cloud Islands):** Alchemy, Enchanting — labor becomes
  abstract/magical
- **Floor 4 (Labyrinth Crypt):** Reckoning — what was combat becomes
  existential
- **Floor 5 (Drowned Library):** Vigil — labor is taken away, only
  presence remains. Mechanically reuses existing systems with new
  flavor; the agency-loss is narrative, not mechanical-engagement-loss.

The acquisition framing is in effect throughout: the player is BEING
GIVEN these skills, not remembering them. The loop is hollowing them
out and replacing them with what the system wants them to be.
